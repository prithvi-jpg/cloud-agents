# The Product Discovery Engine

**Find → Learn → Understand → Dream → Ground**

Everything starts from ambiguity. Not from a feature request, not from a roadmap — from
nothing, from a problem space nobody has resolved into shape yet. The engine is how
ambiguity becomes a product.

Two things to hold before reading further:

1. **Learning is continuous.** It is stage 2 because that is where it dominates, but it
   runs through all five. You are still learning while you ground.
2. **The stages are not bureaucracy.** Each one produces a specific capability you did
   not have before. Skip one, and you carry a hole in your thinking all the way to
   launch — where it shows up as a product that is fine and forgettable.

---

## Stage 1 — FIND (Curiosity, Phase I)

> *"A library is a good thing — you just need to find the right book."*

You are handed ambiguity. What everyone does is find things: find problems, find problem
spaces, find knowledge. *Where can I get information about this? Where is the specific
documentation? Where does all of this live?* You are trying to find the content that will
give you the right information — and the point is not volume. It is finding **the one
right piece that opens everything up.**

### Why this stage is the one that matters most

Finding is what opens curiosity. It is what makes you reach in different directions. If
you begin from someone else's framing of the problem, you inherit their blind spots and
you will never see past them. Finding is how you earn your own framing.

### The move that matters: questions before answers

**Framing the question is the highest-leverage act in the whole engine.** Your question
eventually turns into your answer — so the real work of Find is *finding out what
questions to ask*. What to frame. What is actually being asked underneath the request.

A weak question produces a competent answer to the wrong thing. This is where most
products are lost, and nobody notices because everything downstream looks rigorous.

Write the questions down. Explicitly. Before gathering.

### Where to look

- **UX research and deep human empathy** — truly understanding what the user or customer
  requires. Not what they said in a form.
- **Why here, why now.** Why does this need to exist *in this place, at this moment*?
  Timing is part of the problem definition, not a footnote to it.
- Research papers, articles, news, Reddit threads, forums where people complain honestly
- Company news — what is this company actually facing right now?
- Their business model. How do they make money, who pays, what would threaten them?
  Understand each player from *their own* perspective, not yours.
- The code, the docs, the data, the support tickets
- Calls. Meetings. People.

### Exit criteria

- The research questions are written, not implied
- You can state who the user is, what business they are in, and why this matters now
- You found the *right* source, not merely many sources
- You are curious about something specific that you were not curious about before

### Failure mode

Collecting links as a substitute for thinking. A folder of 40 tabs and no question.
Accepting the problem exactly as it was handed to you.

---

## Stage 2 — LEARN (Curiosity, Phase II)

Once you have the ambiguity *and* the sources, the second half of curiosity begins:
actually learning from them.

Learn **why all of this matters.** Learn from the content. Learn from the code. Learn
from the research questions themselves — not by answering them yet, but by learning what
it would *take* to answer them. The curiosity that made you find the content is the same
curiosity that now has to metabolize it.

This is ingestion with intent. You are not reading to be informed; you are reading to be
changed.

### Exit criteria

- You can explain the domain, the code, the constraints, and the players in your own
  words, sources closed
- You know what you still do not know, specifically
- The vocabulary of the domain is yours now — you can hear when someone is bluffing

### Failure mode

Confusing exposure with learning. Reading everything and retaining a summary. Learning
the surface of a domain well enough to talk, not well enough to build.

---

## Stage 3 — UNDERSTAND (the Eureka moment)

This is the hinge of the entire engine.

> **Learning is not understanding. When you learn something you might also forget it.
> Truly understanding something is when you can make sense of it — when you can actually
> work with it.**

### The recreate test

The test of understanding is recreation.

- If I understand a knowledge graph, I can build a knowledge graph.
- If I understand how GPT works, I can recreate GPT — the way Karpathy wrote one C file
  that just *is* GPT.

Apply the same test to people. If you understand the user, you can **recreate their day.**
Narrate it hour by hour. Name the moment it breaks. Feel the friction.

**If you cannot recreate it, you have not understood it — you have only read about it.**

### Understanding a person means feeling it

Understanding is not analysis. It is feeling:

- How does it *feel* — the pain points, the user stories, the small humiliations
- Their day-to-day core interactions, in sequence
- Where things break — the exact seam
- Where you could improve it *magically*, not incrementally

### What the Eureka moment actually is

You have done the research. Then you make sense of it — and it clicks. Clarity arrives on
its own, and it arrives **with action-drivenness attached**, because of the volume and
quality of data you have put in front of yourself. That is the signal that it is real
understanding: you don't just see it, you want to move.

And in that moment you interconnect all of it — the dots, the research questions, the
feelings, the pain points, the problem space, the resources, the notes, the calls, the
people you met, everything you learned. It stops being a pile and becomes a structure.

### Exit criteria

- You can recreate it: the system, the domain, and the user's day
- You can name the exact break point in the core interaction
- Everything interconnects; the clarity is accompanied by an urge to act
- You feel it, not just describe it

### Failure mode

Declaring the Eureka before it arrives, because the deadline says stage 3 is over. A
false Eureka feels like a good summary. A real one feels like pressure to build.

**Do not proceed without this. Every stage after it is built on this floor.**

---

## Stage 4 — DREAM (intuitive simulation)

The instant understanding lands, intuition kicks in — your life flashes, and you already
know you have to dream. To see where this can go. How far it can reach. How much impact
it can create.

**That is truly the crux of anything. Dreaming is what humans are made of.**

### Dreaming is not idea-having

An idea is not a dream. Dreaming is:

**idea → vision → mission**

You take the idea, you give it a **vision**, and having a vision gives you a **mission to
complete**. Without the vision you have a feature. Without the mission you have a poster.

### Simulate

Then run the simulation. See it play out:

- What must be true to get there — every step
- **Second- and third-order effects** of introducing this
- Competitors: how do they respond? What does the market do?
- Different market conditions, different segments
- Inside the product: if you help them here, what else changes?
- What new risks are you creating? What could this break?

### Dream big

The goal is the incredible innovation — that state of mind, the bliss of *this is under
control, I am solving this*, that magical intuitiveness that cannot be described once you
solve it. **That click has to come.** If the dream does not produce it, dream bigger.

### Exit criteria

- Vision and mission are both stated, and they are different from each other
- The simulation is written out, not vibes: steps, responses, and effects
- 2nd- and 3rd-order effects named, including the ones that hurt
- Competitive and risk response mapped
- The click is present

### Failure mode

Dreaming small because grounding feels safer. Or dreaming in a vacuum — a vision with no
simulation, which is just optimism. Both produce roadmaps nobody believes in.

---

## Stage 5 — GROUND TO REALITY (tactical execution)

Once the dream is set: **okay — let's ground it.**

By now you know everyone. The problems, the users, the customer, the market analysis, the
comparative analysis. You have the data. Now you do actual product sense, product
management, product building, product design.

### Come to the present moment

Grounding means the **present second**. With everything going on right now, in this
market, with these constraints:

**What can you do at this exact second that disrupts the market, brings you back in the
game, brings distribution, and puts customers right at your foot?**

Not what you could do with twenty engineers next year. Now.

### Crack one case, then build the stack

Once you crack that one particular thing, you can build a stack around it and envision
outward from there. **But the fact is you have to start somewhere — and that is grounding
to reality.** The wedge is not a compromise on the dream; it is the only door into it.

### What grounding produces

- **Decisions**, taken with all the data now in view
- **The story** — framed to be convincing. It has to make sense at the end of the day.
  You simulate, and you see this is what is going to happen and this is how it plays out.
- **Game theory.** It is all game theory at the end of the day. A Beautiful Mind — seeing
  your imagination play out across the other players' moves.
- **Design.** Something designed *at this moment* that cracks the same feeling you got at
  the Eureka and in the dream. If the design does not carry that feeling, it is not done.
- **Prioritization with a reason.** There are many things you could build, for many
  people, across many verticals. **Why are you prioritizing this one?** Think through
  what others have done here, and build for that. This is where product judgment actually
  lives.

### Think differently

To beat the crowd you have to think out of the box — innovative in all directions,
creative enough to present designs that land. Doing what everyone else is doing produces
what everyone else has.

### Exit criteria

- One thing to build now, named, scoped, buildable
- Explicit reason why this and not the alternatives
- A convincing story a stranger would believe
- The game theory: what the other players do in response
- A design that carries the Eureka feeling
- The stack it unlocks, sketched

### Failure mode

Grounding so hard the dream evaporates — an MVP with no soul, indistinguishable from what
exists. Or never grounding: a beautiful vision with no first move, which ships nothing.

---

## The loop, compressed

If you have five minutes instead of five weeks, still do this in order:

1. **Find** — what is the real question here?
2. **Learn** — what do I need to know to answer it?
3. **Understand** — can I narrate their day and name the break?
4. **Dream** — where does this go, and what happens second-order?
5. **Ground** — what is the one thing to build now, and why this one?

Five minutes run in order beats five weeks run out of order.
