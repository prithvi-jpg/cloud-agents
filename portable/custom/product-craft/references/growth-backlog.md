# Growth Backlog & The Assembled Operating Loop

---

## Part A — The honest syllabus

These are the explicitly-named growth areas. They are stated here so the agent can
**actively compensate for them** — when working on something that touches one of these,
apply extra rigor and make the reasoning visible rather than assuming it.

### 1. Second-order thinking

> *"I'm hoping to become a natural at second-order thinking."*

**Practice:** for every product decision, write the first-order effect, then ask *"and then
what happens?"* **twice more.**

```
We ship X
  → first-order:  users do Y
    → second-order: which means Z changes for [other party / the business / behavior over time]
      → third-order: which means ...
```

**Force the second and third order explicitly in any recommendation.** Include at least
one effect that is *bad* — a decision with only good downstream effects has not been
thought through.

### 2. Forming hypotheses and research questions

Bridging UXR instinct with data-science hypothesis discipline. Started; keep drilling.
**Practice:** rewrite every research question until it names variables and an expected
relationship, and could be proven wrong. See `research-craft.md`.

### 3. Problem selection judgment

> *"How do we know what kind of problem brings out the impact that is important to solve?"*

The open question. Working heuristics to apply and refine:

- **Count the humans.** Impact quantified beats impact asserted.
- **Proximity to root cause.** Solving upstream collapses many symptoms at once.
- **Silence signal.** Problems people stopped complaining about and adapted to.
- **Wedge potential.** Does solving this open a stack, or is it terminal?
- **Frequency × pain.** Daily friction beats rare catastrophe for retention; the reverse
  for trust.
- **Who else can solve this?** If five companies can, it is not your problem to solve.

**When making a prioritization call, say which heuristic decided it.** That is how the
judgment gets trained instead of just exercised.

### 4. Building your own frameworks

> *"I see that companies create frameworks to guide practice, aligned with broader product
> goals and vision. Strategic thinking: build your own toolbox."*

**This skill is the first entry in that toolbox.** It is meant to be edited. When a project
teaches something new — a heuristic that worked, a failure mode that recurred — write it
back into the relevant reference file. A framework that never changes is a framework
nobody is actually using.

### 5. Optimal-experience instincts

> *"Get a summarized take on the best insights humans have found to make optimal
> experiences... and be a natural in applying them."*

Applying known HCI/psychology principles fluently rather than deriving them each time:
flow, peak-end rule, cognitive load, Fitts's/Hick's laws, recognition over recall, the
generation effect, defaults as design decisions. When designing an interaction, name which
principle is being leaned on.

### 6. AI + AR collective intelligence

The standing bet that *"the mother of all demos comes back with holistic AI and AR
augmenting collective intelligence."* Keep collecting evidence **for and against.**

## Part B — The assembled operating loop

The whole craft, compressed into one repeatable cycle:

```
Notice   →  sketchbook, love/hate lists, invisible problems
Frame    →  problem statement with root cause + JTBD + "who cares?"
Understand → interviews with silence and "why", psychographics, saturation
Decide   →  hypothesis, 100 > 10 > 1, prioritize by root-cause proximity
Build    →  prototype for instant feedback; read-only before trust
Measure  →  HEART / North Star defined before launch; guardrails named
Reflect  →  second-order effects; what did I miss; update this document
```

**Relationship to the Discovery Engine:** the engine (Find → Learn → Understand → Dream →
Ground) is how you go from *nothing* to a direction. This loop is how you go from a
direction to a *shipped, measured, improved* thing. The engine runs first, and re-runs
whenever the problem space shifts under you.

**The Reflect step is the one that compounds.** It is also the one most often skipped,
because by then the thing has shipped and attention has moved. Everything else in this
skill improves only if Reflect actually happens — including this skill, which should be
edited when reality disagrees with it.

## The five questions to ask of anything you are building

1. **Who cares, and how many?** (impact, quantified)
2. **What is the intent — and can the user feel it?**
3. **What is the job to be done — and what are they choosing instead** (including
   spreadsheets)?
4. **What earns trust before I ask for action?**
5. **What happens after this works — and then what happens after that?**
