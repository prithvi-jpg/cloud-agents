# Research Craft — thinking like an HCI researcher

---

## Questions are the atomic unit

The hardest and most important skill: **developing research questions.** The bridge
between UXR instinct and data-science rigor is this:

> *"The formulation of a hypothesis clearly states the parameters of the experiment and
> communicates the expected relationship."*

**The synthesis: research questions are hypothesis-shaped curiosity.**

A good research question:
- **Names the variables**
- **States the expected relationship** between them
- **Is falsifiable** — there is an outcome that would prove it wrong

Treat every RQ as falsifiable. If no observation could contradict it, it is a topic, not a
question, and it will produce a report full of themes and no decisions.

**Weak:** "How do users feel about the onboarding flow?"
**Strong:** "Does replacing the numeric input with a preference-based control reduce
first-session abandonment, and for which segment?"

The weak one cannot be wrong. That is the tell.

## Methods practiced

### Grounded Theory: open + axial coding with memos

The core synthesis method:
> *"Compiled and open-coded interviews, clubbed codes into categories, formed relationships
> to bring together insights through stories."*

The sequence: **open code** (label what is there, without a schema) → **cluster into
categories** → **axial code** (form relationships between categories) → **memo throughout**
(your thinking about the data is data) → **surface insight as story**, because stories
transfer and code lists do not.

### The rest of the kit

| Method | Gives you |
|---|---|
| **Affinity mapping** | Bottom-up structure from unstructured notes |
| **Card sorting** (hierarchical clustering) | The user's mental model of your information architecture |
| **Conjoint / MaxDiff** | What they actually trade off, not what they claim to value |
| **Surveys** | Breadth — *"big picture quickly, geographically dispersed"* |
| **Interviews** | Depth — the why behind the breadth |
| **Co-design workshops** | Users as design partners, not subjects |
| **Behavioral + sentiment data at scale** | What happened, plus how it felt, across the whole base |

### Co-design workshop moves

- **Role-play with a human "agent"** — a person plays the system. Wizard-of-Oz for
  intelligent behavior, which is the only way to prototype an agent before it exists.
- **Blue Sky sketching** — surface hopes. What is the best version of this future?
- **Black Mirror sketching** — surface fears. What is the worst? This one is
  underused and it is where the real design constraints come from, especially for AI.

### Mixed methods

Qual → quant conversion: qualitative depth generates the hypotheses and the language;
quantitative breadth sizes them. The direction matters — quant first tells you *what* is
happening with no idea *why*, and teams then invent the why.

### Research repository culture

> *"Insights cited elsewhere; doc culture — hour-long meetings debating the report like a
> writer's room."*

Research that is not cited elsewhere did not land. The measure of a study is whether other
people's decisions reference it months later. Debate the report seriously — like a writer's
room, not a status update.

## The why behind the method

> *"Have curiosity. Understand the mental models, the why, their pain points, how they
> think of and interact with products... This is important because IT IMPACTS THIS NUMBER
> OF PEOPLE."*

**The research stance: every study must end at impact, quantified — not just themes.**

A findings deck that ends at "users experience friction during setup" has not finished. It
finishes at: *this affects N users, costing X, and here is the decision it changes.*

Themes are the middle of the work. The number is the end of it.

## Research plan checklist

- [ ] Research questions written, variable-naming, falsifiable
- [ ] Method matched to the question (depth vs. breadth vs. tradeoff vs. structure)
- [ ] Recruitment criteria based on **behavior**, not demographics
- [ ] Interview guide checked for leading / double-barreled / hypothetical / forced-choice
- [ ] Saturation target set (~15 min, 20–30 ideal), triangulation planned
- [ ] Analysis method chosen in advance (open → axial → categories → story)
- [ ] Impact quantification planned: how will findings convert to a number of people?
- [ ] Ethics: what could this research surface that harms a participant? What could the
      resulting design make easier that should stay hard?
- [ ] Where the insight will live so it gets cited later
