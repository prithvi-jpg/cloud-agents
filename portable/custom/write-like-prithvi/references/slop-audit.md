# Slop audit

Use anti-slop rules to protect Prithvi's voice, not to replace it with sanitized prose.

Copyedit exception: when the selected operation is Fix only, do not run the four-question gate, contextual tell scan, portability test, or voice redesign. A generic-sounding word is not a correctness error. Apply only the hard correctness and format rules relevant to the source, then stop.

## Hard failures

Fix every occurrence unless it appears inside a direct quotation or exact source value:

- an em dash in Prithvi's own prose;
- invented facts, figures, dates, quotes, anecdotes, memories, feelings, relationships, or outcomes;
- leaked tool or citation placeholders;
- an unfinished placeholder in a finished draft;
- a broken format contract, including a tweet over 240 characters or a tweet containing a hashtag or emoji.

## Four-question gate

Test each sentence or short paragraph:

1. **Interchangeable:** Could this exact line appear in twenty unrelated pieces? Replace it with a specific detail, mechanism, consequence, judgment, or cut it.
2. **Additive:** Does it add information, stance, emotion, or useful rhythm? Cut restatement and padding.
3. **Shaped:** Did the content create this structure, or did a template impose it? Break repeated layouts, uniform paragraph sizes, canned intro-body-summary shapes, and unnecessary lists.
4. **Voiced:** Does it fit Prithvi's register for this destination? Rewrite toward the voice profile, never toward neutral assistant prose.

## Contextual tell clusters

Treat one occurrence as a prompt to inspect, not an automatic rewrite. Rewrite when several signals cluster or the line fails the four-question gate.

### Language

- inflated verbs and adjectives such as leverage, utilize, foster, robust, seamless, transformative, groundbreaking, pivotal, nuanced, comprehensive, or innovative when they replace a fact;
- empty setup such as "here's the thing," "it's worth noting," "at its core," "in today's world," or "let's dive in";
- importance labels such as "this changes everything," "a testament to," "marks a pivotal moment," or "plays a vital role";
- unnamed attribution such as "experts agree," "studies show," or "many believe";
- fake-strong verbs such as "serves as" when "is," "has," or a direct verb is clearer;
- repeated softeners such as just, actually, really, simply, honestly, or fundamentally when they carry no meaning.

Keep a flagged word when it is literal, technically precise, quoted, a proper name, or genuinely part of the source voice.

### Structure

- repeated "not X, but Y" contrasts;
- rule-of-three cadence used by reflex rather than content;
- fake question pivots such as "The best part?";
- colon reveals and clickbait headings;
- synonym cycling for the same object;
- stacked dramatic fragments;
- identical sentence or paragraph shapes;
- reflexive bullets, bold emphasis, headings, or tables where plain prose would read better;
- recap endings, moral extraction, aphorism closers, and engagement questions.

### Stance

- fake enthusiasm;
- automatic agreement with the prompt;
- false balance where evidence supports a direct answer;
- no real position or consequence;
- explaining the joke, emotion, or implication;
- restating the source in smoother words and calling it insight;
- covering every angle when the reader needs one answer.

## Positive replacements

- Replace an adjective with the fact that earned it.
- Replace a vague claim with a name, number, date, moment, mechanism, or consequence already supplied.
- Repeat the clear noun instead of cycling synonyms.
- Use active subjects and direct verbs.
- Keep honest hedges when uncertainty is real. Remove blanket hedging around established facts.
- Let a paragraph stop when it is done.
- Preserve useful roughness from the source. Never manufacture errors as a human costume.

## Depth

### Quick gate

Use for tweets, messages, and routine email:

1. Verify the format contract.
2. Remove hard failures.
3. Run the four questions once.
4. Read aloud once.

### Full gate

Use for public, creative, consequential, or long-form work:

1. Identify three to five voice signals from the source and profile.
2. Mark the concrete particulars that must survive.
3. Run the four questions.
4. Scan contextual clusters.
5. Check that the opening is specific and the ending adds new work rather than recapping.
6. Read aloud in the intended register.
7. Fix only failed spans and rerun once.

Stop after two passes. If it still feels generic, the problem is missing thought, evidence, or voice context. Do not keep polishing.
